## Item 1 — Exterior once-over: fix the "darker box", make the outside a pleasing, logical picture
Deep-dive: spin up a dedicated subagent to review the baked vista + the whole outside and produce a
revised composition plan, then implement. C6-diagnosed root causes (verify each):

### 1a. The "darker box" around the building
The vista canvas is drawn with a hard start at the building's outer wall — the bright meadow meets the
warm interior floor abruptly, so the building reads as a dark rectangle/box floating in the meadow.
Diagnosed cause: there is no ground transition band between the building footprint and the vista meadow.
Fix: add a soft transition zone right outside every exterior wall — a ~1–2 tile band of a blend (concrete
path / grass edging / gravel apron / terracotta paving that matches the building's exterior wall color),
with a soft shadow that the building casts onto the ground (consistent sun direction: shadows fall the
same way as the exterior sunbeams). The building should look *planted in* the meadow, not floating.
Verify with a screenshot: at default zoom the transition is visible and the box is gone.

### 1b. The outside picture — pleasing AND logical
Go through every exterior element (mountains, brook, dock, bench, cairn, stepping stones, logs, dock,
fairy ring, sunbeams, hawks, mist, trees) and ask: does the COMPOSITION read as a calm, logical place?
- The brook should flow in a sensible direction (e.g. from a mountain gap down to the tarn, not through
  the middle of a meadow for no reason); the dock should sit at the tarn; the trail/signpost should lead
  from the building to a logical destination; the bench should face the view (north peaks) not the wall;
  the fire pit shouldn't be adjacent to a flower bed.
- Add 1–2 connective tissue elements if needed (a winding path from the building door to the bench/dock,
  a small bridge where the brook crosses).
- Keep the day palette and the seeded determinism. Keep it cheap (pre-baked + few animated accents).
- Subagent delivers: a list of every exterior element with its current position, a keep/adjust/remove
  verdict with a one-line reason, and the final composition. Paste that table in the report.

## Item 2 — Lighting: fewer, larger, logical lights; less "fog"
Current: `map.js initLighting()` hardcodes 3–4 overlapping ~3.5-tile warm glows per room (desk + corner
+ entry + counter/table), ambient floor baked at 0.62, cosine falloff ~3.5 tiles. The overlap of many
small warm glows = the "foggy" muddled interior, and lights at doors/entries are illogical.
Fix:
- Fewer, larger lights per room: 1–2 per room (a main overhead + at most one accent like a desk lamp).
  Radius ~5–7 tiles, lower per-light alpha so the total warm wash is gentler and more even.
- Place lights where a real office would have them: overhead above the desk, over the table, over the
  counter — NOT at the door, NOT in empty corners.
- Reduce the "fog": lower the baked light-canvas alpha (the `tint`/gradient alphas), keep the ambient
  floor a touch higher (less dark pooling), so the room reads clearly lit and even, not hazy.
- Keep `N` fullbright toggle working. Keep the lighting study: paste before/after numbers + screenshots.

## Item 4 — Glass meeting room (private voice) + whiteboard + typing board + projector/docking
This is the biggest item. Add a new room (reclaim/reshape space — prefer carving a new enclosed area; if
the map is full, resize the map grid — keep every existing target in `test/reachability-test.js` passing

### 4a. The room
- A small enclosed meeting room with one door, and glass walls on at least 2–3 sides (semi-transparent
  so you can see the room from outside, but it's clearly a room).
- The door can be CLOSED (a toggle — walk to it and press the door key, or a small "close/open" affordance).
- **Private voice:** when the door is CLOSED, a player inside is only audible to other players who are
  inside the meeting room (server-side room check on the `va` relay: sender-in-closed-meeting-room ⇒
  only deliver to receivers also in the meeting room). When OPEN, normal 800px proximity + wall
  occlusion apply as today. Show a small "🔇 private" chip when a player is inside the closed room.

### 4b. Glass whiteboard
- A whiteboard mounted on a glass wall in the room. Click it (or walk up + E) to draw: mouse/touch
  strokes in a chosen color (small palette), a clear button, and an eraser.
- Multiplayer: strokes sync over WS (broadcast stroke events), so everyone sees the drawing. Keep the
  whiteboard content per-room, alive for the session (server holds the current stroke list; on join a
  client fetches the current board). Cap stroke history (e.g. last 500 points) so it doesn't bloat.

### 4c. Typing board
- A small board you click to focus, then type on: each keypress appends a character (rendered as a grid
  of cells, like a little ledger). Backspace deletes, Enter moves a line.
- Sync over WS like the whiteboard (append/delete events). One shared typing board in the room (or one
  per side of the table — pick and document).

### 4d. Projector + dock + screen share
- A projector at the room's far wall projects onto a screen/canvas in the room.
- A single "dock" at the table: one player docks at a time (server-enforced: second player gets a "busy"

  stylized "display panel" on the projector that shows the docked player's current view/last-action

  caption) — NOT a literal OS screen-capture (that's out of scope for a zero-dep canvas game; the
  projector shows a stylized share panel with the docked player's name + a small live-updating summary
  like their room and recent action). Undock by walking away or pressing the dock again.
